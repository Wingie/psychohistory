# Round 2: the independent referee report on LOGOS v0.2

**Audit date:** 2026-07-25. **Stance:** adversarial, and independent of the process that wrote the paper.
**Scope:** `logos.tex` v0.2, `logos/ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md`, `logos/BIBLIOGRAPHY_REVIEW.md`, `logos/GAPS.md`, `logos/LOGOS_HARNESS.md`, `README.md`, and the psychohistory validation suite the harness names as its adjudicator.
**Method:** every finding below was written by one pass and then handed to a second pass whose only job was to refute it. Findings that did not survive were dropped. Findings that survived in weakened form were downgraded and the weakening is recorded inline. Where the refutation pass corrected a number, the corrected number is what appears here.

## 0. What round 2 found that round 1 did not, and why

`ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md` and `BIBLIOGRAPHY_REVIEW.md` were written by the same authoring process that wrote `logos.tex`. They are self-review. They are also good self-review: F-01, F-03, F-06 and F-07 are real fixes, verified below, and the disclosure discipline they demanded was actually applied in the paper body rather than only in the review documents. This report is not a claim that round 1 was lazy. It is a claim that round 1 was structurally unable to catch four classes of defect, and that all four are present.

**Class one: a citation that resolves, is real, is quoted accurately, and supports the opposite of the inference drawn from it.** `BIBLIOGRAPHY_REVIEW.md:3` states its method as "every citation in the source draft was checked against a primary source", and `BIBLIOGRAPHY_REVIEW.md:24` claims every quoted arXiv ID resolves. Both are true. Neither catches C-02, where both debate papers are cited for a diversity claim that both explicitly disclaim, in a sentence printed in the abstract. That finding is CRITICAL and it collapses the only architectural item in the paper's own list of three original contributions.

**Class two: a claim with no citation at all.** A method that checks citations against sources cannot check a claim that has none. `logos.tex:94`, the token-supply premise the entire data wall rests on, carries no `\cite` and no number; `logos.tex:172`, one of only two enumerated motivations for the tower split, asserts a measurement with no source. `BIBLIOGRAPHY_REVIEW.md` §6 lists eleven such items and neither of these two.

**Class three: a derivation round 1 reproduced rather than audited.** `ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md:44-48` (F-01) uses the identical expert-fraction method for `N_act` that A-01 shows is wrong by 1.5x to 1.8x on the only two shipped models where ground truth exists. The self-review's fix is correct; its input is not.

**Class four: an entire artefact round 1 never opened.** `grep -n -i 'substrate b|psychohistory|validation/' logos/ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md` returns zero matches. The psychohistory validation suite is the adjudicator on which `LOGOS_HARNESS.md` §4 and falsifier F9 depend, and it had received no referee pass before this one. Round 2 opened it and found the sealed pass's headline p-value of 1.7e-7 rests on an assumed null fire rate of 0.10 that the repository itself measured, twice, at roughly 0.80, and that the single cleanest measurement of it was computed and then dropped one function return before serialisation.

One finding runs the other way. Round 1's own `BIBLIOGRAPHY_REVIEW.md:98` declares a figure untraceable while citing, two sentences earlier, the abstract that contains it. C-03 reverses that call in the paper's favour.

Forty-six findings survived. Two are CRITICAL, six are HIGH. Twenty are LOW, and a LOW finding here means a real, demonstrated defect with no downstream consequence, which is the correct grade for most of what is left. Section 6 lists what round 2 checked and found sound, which is a large fraction of the paper and is the honest counterweight to a raw finding count.

## 2. CRITICAL

### C-02 · Both debate papers are cited for a conclusion they explicitly refute
*(round-1 stream ID: citations R2-02)*

**Location:** `logos.tex:32`, `:541`, `:554`, `:561`; `logos/LOGOS_HARNESS.md:33`, `:35`, `:58`.

**Claim as written.** `logos.tex:541`: "Deliberation among parties who already know the same things reinforces a shared prior rather than correcting it." `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:35`: "Consequence for this spec: the towers must be informatively different, or the loop is provably worthless." `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:58`, Tier-C claim #2, flagged "This is the claim we would most like tested": "The tower architecture supplies the diversity the martingale theorem requires. Result (1) says improvement needs participants who know different things."

**Why it is wrong.** Neither cited source says diversity is what breaks the martingale, and both say things that contradict the inference. (a) arXiv:2601.19921's Theorem 1 proves that calibrated-confidence-weighted debate among agents that are explicitly *homogeneous* and fully connected yields a strict submartingale: expected correctness strictly increases, with no informational diversity and no external observation. (b) That paper's diversity result (Proposition 1) does not break the martingale; it only shifts the initial state distribution, and the paper states the dynamics "remain a martingale". (c) Choi et al., the actual source of the martingale theorem, explicitly extend it to heterogeneous agents. So "improvement needs participants who know different things" is precisely what the result does not say, and the mechanism that does break the martingale in the literature is an internal protocol change, not an external observation channel.

**Demonstration.** From the extracted 2601.19921v3 PDF: "Theorem 1 (Confidence-weighted debate yields a submartingale). Assume that: (i) agents are homogeneous and fully connected; and (ii) confidence is positively correlated with correctness... Then the belief process {p_{i,t}}_{t>=0} becomes a strict submartingale: E[p_{i,t} | F_{t-1}] >= p_{i,t-1}, with strict inequality on a set of positive probability." And: "confidence-modulated debate breaks the martingale symmetry of vanilla MAD and, in expectation, strictly improves correctness over rounds." On diversity, Appendix D verbatim: "For diversity injection, we are not changing the dynamics of the martingale process, but increases the prior probability of success. For confidence-modulated debate, the incorporation of a confidence score that is positively correlated with correctness breaks martingale process into submartingale." And §D.2: "Note that all improvement arises from a better initial distribution over F1... Diversity-aware initialization operates before this point." The published abstract says the same: "diversity-aware initialisation improves the prior probability of MAD success without changing the underlying update dynamics." From the extracted 2508.17536 PDF (Choi et al., §5 / App D.4): "we introduce the notion of 'collective intelligence' and demonstrate that it forms a martingale process even with heterogeneous agents." Choi et al. Table 4 (heterogeneous personas, Qwen2.5-7B, GSM8K) matched cell by cell against Table 1 (homogeneous, same model, same benchmark): Decentralized T=2 +8.34, T=3 +8.00, T=5 +9.00; Sparse T=2 +1.33, T=3 +1.66, T=5 +1.33; Centralized T=2 +13.67 points. Mean +6.19 points **in favour of identical agents**.

The most damaging instance is in the abstract. `logos.tex:32`: "The theorem on debate says that diversity of information is what breaks the martingale, and towers trained on different corpora under different objectives supply exactly that diversity." That is the exact negation of what the cited source proves, printed in the abstract. The same false premise appears at `logos.tex:561` ("The theorem's condition for improvement is that participants hold informatively different views") and at `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:58`.

**Why CRITICAL.** `logos.tex:557` names exactly three original contributions. The second, the only architectural one, flagged at `:561` and `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:58` as "the claim we would most like tested", rests entirely on a reading of the theorem that the theorem's own authors explicitly disclaim.

**One overstatement recorded.** The refutation pass found that Zhu et al. buy calibrated confidence with external supervision (extracted appendix: "GRPO Confidence Calibration Setup, LoRA (r=64, alpha=32)" and "we manually curated a harder subset of data on which model accuracy was closer to 50%"), so the confidence route is not free of exogenous signal. That is a real steelman for `logos.tex:554`. It does not rescue `:32` or `:561`, whose defect is the diversity claim.

**Fix.** Rewrite `logos.tex:541` to state what the sources state: under homogeneous agents with unweighted belief updates the debate belief process is a martingale (Choi et al., arXiv:2508.17536), and the martingale extends to heterogeneous agents; the known ways to break it are protocol-internal (confidence weighting, Zhu et al. 2601.19921 Thm 1) or better initialisation, not diversity as such. Fix `logos.tex:32` and `:561` identically. Then either withdraw `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:58` Tier-C claim #2 or restate it as a conjecture that explicitly contradicts Choi et al.'s heterogeneous-agent extension, and add a falsifier: if calibrated-confidence weighting alone lifts ensemble accuracy without any environment adjudication, the observation bound is not what limits the loop.

### P-01 · The assumed null p0 = 0.10 is refuted by the repository's own data
*(round-1 stream ID: psychohistory R1-01)*

**Location:** `validation/neff_v4/roster_v4.py:53` (`BINOM_P0 = 0.10`); `PRE_REGISTRATION_neff_v4.md:57-61`; refuting data in `validation/reddit_wsb/result_wsb_neff.json` and `validation/neff_v3/derive_f_v3.json`.

**Claim as written.** "Under H0 ('the partition carries no community-specific structure'), the real partition is exchangeable with its shuffles, so each event fires with probability p0 = 0.10 (it exceeds its own 90th-percentile shuffle by chance one time in ten)." (`PRE_REGISTRATION_neff_v4.md:57-61`) and "p0 = 0.10 is the construction-implied null fire rate" (`neff_v4/RESULTS.md:20-23`).

**Why it is wrong.** p0 = 0.10 would be correct only if the observed statistic were exchangeable with the 300 block-label shuffles. It is not: the observed drop uses a modularity-optimised Louvain partition (`neff_collapse_wsb.py:125`), while every null draw is a random relabelling. Random blocks are near-identical mixtures of the same users, so their mean-normalised activity series co-move, N_eff stays near 1 in both the baseline and onset windows, and the null drop concentrates on zero. The correct null fire rate is not a construction constant; it is an empirical quantity, and p0 is doing 100% of the work.

**Demonstration.** `math.comb` reproduces the published figure exactly: P(X>=9 | n=12, p0=0.10) = 1.658350e-07, matching `result_neff_v4.json` `summary.binom_p_ge_k`. Recomputing the frozen rule at empirically plausible p0: P(X>=9 | n=12, p0=0.80) = 0.7945689; at p0=0.833 it is 0.8741103. Bisection puts the break of pass condition (b) (P < 0.01) at p0 = 0.37781. Two empirical estimates of p0 exist in the repository. First, `result_wsb_neff.json` contains a matched CALM (non-event) arm through the identical specificity test: 9 of 10 events fire and 8 of 10 CALM windows fire, Fisher exact p = 1.0. Second, and this is the uncontaminated one, v3's 12 genuinely-quiet clean windows: `derive_f_v3.json.route_i_clean_null.clean_drops_sorted = [-0.2028, -0.1536, 0.0235, 0.0442, 0.0565, 0.0724, 0.1235, 0.2275, 0.2719, 0.3200, 0.3649, 0.4286]`, of which 10 of 12 exceed the median v4 shuffle-null p90 of 0.0136828, giving a quiet-window fire rate of 0.83.

Independent confirmation of the mechanism, added by the refutation pass: re-implementing `neff_macro` and `_collapse_from_user_mats` (`neff_collapse_wsb.py:149-224`) and running 20 trials of synthetic WSB-shaped data with **no cascade of any kind** gave a median shuffle-null p90 of 0.0034, the same order as the v4 events' 0.00279 to 0.01792, and the fire rule still fired 5 of 20 times. The null p90 is degenerate whether or not a cascade occurred, so p0 cannot be a construction constant. Sharper still: in v4, `fires` agrees with the pure sign test `drop_macro > 0` in 11 of 12 events, and on the v3 clean quiet windows 10 of 12 have `drop_macro > 0`.

**Two corrections against the round-1 draft of this finding, recorded.** (1) The clause "the repo already measured it, twice, and never reported it" is false for the calm arm: `reddit_wsb/RESULTS.md:141-142` states "So 8 of 10 'calm' windows are not calm and themselves fire". The number was published, and the repo attributes it to contamination, a defence that holds for much of that arm (`market_selloff_jan2022`'s calm window is 2021-01-24, the GameStop squeeze itself). (2) Therefore p0 = 0.80 and the corrected p = 0.79 are not established as point values. The weight of this finding rests on the uncontaminated v3 clean arm, where the fire rate was never published; see P-03.

**Why CRITICAL anyway.** 1.7e-7 is the sole quantitative support for the SEALED PASS and is carried into `psychohistory.tex:769`, `:781` and `:787` as "the program's most rigorous positive to date". p0 = 0.10 is arithmetically valid only against the straw H0 the pre-registration writes, which the repo itself asserts is false of WSB at all times (`reddit_wsb/RESULTS.md:118-130` argues WSB fires precisely because it has community structure where Wikipedia does not). Condition (b) fails above p0 = 0.378 and the repo's own clean-null data cannot exclude that range.

**Fix.** Retract the "binomial p = 1.7e-7" figure from `README.md:16,95`, `RUN_AND_CHECK.md:186,192`, `NEFF_COLLAPSE_SYNTHESIS.md:55-56,109`, `PRE_REGISTRATION.md:306,325` and `psychohistory.tex:787`. Replace the construction-implied p0 with an empirically calibrated one: re-run `analyze_v4`-equivalent code over at least 30 genuinely-quiet WSB pseudo-onsets, record `fires_vs_shuffle`, and use the measured rate as p0. Or drop the binomial entirely and report the event-versus-quiet-window fire-rate contrast with a Fisher or permutation test, which is the actual comparison of interest.

## 3. HIGH

### A-01 · N_act = 5e10 is derived by a method demonstrably wrong on both models with ground truth
*(round-1 stream ID: arithmetic R2-02)*

**Location:** `logos.tex:106` (§2.2); Table 1 row 3 (`logos.tex:128`) and row 4 (`:129`); §13.2 R1/R2 (`:619-620`). `ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md:44-48` (F-01) uses the identical derivation.

**Claim as written.** "Kimi K3 activates 16 of 896 experts, placing it near $5\times10^{10}$ active parameters against $2.8\times10^{12}$ total, about 98\% sparsity \cite{moonshot2026k3,huang2026k3}."

**Why it is wrong.** "Placing it near" is doing the work of a source: the figure is computed as (16/896) x 2.8e12, which returns exactly 5.0000e10. That method assumes 100% of parameters are routed-expert parameters. In every real MoE the always-active set (attention, embeddings, the LM head, the shared experts) is a large fraction of the active count and is excluded from the routed-expert fraction. The paper's own cited primary source contradicts it: the vLLM K3 preview says "16 active per token plus shared experts", and shared experts fire on every token. The figure is the single input to Proposition 1's headline number, to Table 1 rows 3 and 4, and to the entire R1/R2 reading of the EU AI Act threshold, and it is derived rather than sourced by a method that is measurably biased low.

**Demonstration.** Ground truth from primary sources, then the same naive method applied to them. Kimi K2 (HF model card `moonshotai/Kimi-K2-Instruct`, fetched): 1T total, 32B activated, 384 experts, 8 selected, 1 shared. Naive gives (8/384) x 1e12 = 2.0833e10 against an actual 3.200e10, understating by 1.5360x. DeepSeek-V3 (arXiv:2412.19437 abstract plus `config.json`, fetched): 671B total, 37B activated, `n_routed_experts=256`, `num_experts_per_tok=8`, `n_shared_experts=1`. Naive gives (8/256) x 671e9 = 2.0969e10 against an actual 3.700e10, understating by 1.7645x. Reconstructing why from V3's config (61 layers, `first_k_dense_replace=3`, hidden 7168, `moe_intermediate` 2048, intermediate 18432): active FFN params = 3 x 3x7168x18432 + 58 x (8+1)x3x7168x2048 = 2.4178e10, so the always-on MLA attention plus embeddings plus head account for 3.700e10 - 2.4178e10 = 1.2822e10, i.e. **34.65% of active parameters, all of it invisible to the expert fraction**.

Independent corroboration of the method being wrong, from a source the first pass did not lean on. photoncap.net (fetched): "A simple 2.8T x 16/896 calculation produces about 50B, but that arithmetic assumes the parameters are spread uniformly across routed experts... In reality the 2.8T total also includes always-active attention, embeddings, routing, and shared components, so the correct expression is common parameters plus 16/896 of the routed-expert parameters." northflank.com (fetched, `what-is-kimi-k3-self-hosting`): "Moonshot has not published a single active-parameter figure comparable to the 32 billion active parameters specified for Kimi K2.6."

**Magnitude, corrected.** Solving the correction factor analytically, f = (c/T)/(k/n) + (1 - c/T) where c is the always-on common set: V3 has c/T = 2.55% and k/n = 3.125% giving f = 1.79; K2 has c/T = 1.14% and k/n = 2.083% giving f = 1.54. K3's k/n = 1.7857% is smaller, which amplifies f for a given c/T, but K3's c/T is unknown and likely lower at 2.8T total. ### A-02 · The routing-efficiency metric eta has four defects, three of them real
*(round-1 stream ID: arithmetic R2-05)*

**Location:** `logos.tex:496-502` (Eq. 11, §11.2); relied on by falsifier F10 (`logos.tex:695`) and by `GAPS.md:35` and `:66`.

**Claim as written.** "$\eta = \frac{S_{\text{router}} - S_{\text{best single tower}}}{S_{\text{oracle}} - S_{\text{best single tower}}}$ ... At $\eta = 0$ the router adds nothing over picking one tower and keeping it. At $\eta = 1$ it routes perfectly." And `logos.tex:502`: "$\eta$ must be reported per domain."

**Why it is wrong.** This is proposed as the metric that makes the Mixture-of-Towers design falsifiable, and F10's criterion is stated purely in terms of it. (1) The denominator is zero whenever the best single tower solves every item any tower solves, and the paper's own mandate to report eta per domain shrinks n and drives that probability up. (2) Nothing bounds eta below, and no scale is given on which to interpret a negative value. (3) The floor is a post-hoc maximum over k noisy tower scores on the same evaluation set, so it is biased high and eta biased low, with the bias growing in the number of towers, meaning the metric penalises exactly the wide ensembles it exists to evaluate. (4) The oracle ceiling counts an item as solvable if any tower is right, including by guessing, so on multiple-choice evaluations it manufactures headroom out of chance.

**Demonstration.** Monte Carlo, 3000-20000 trials each, pure Python, seed 0, rerun independently by the refutation pass. (1) Denominator == 0: three towers at 0.90/0.20/0.20 on an n=50 per-domain slice, P(S_oracle == S_best_single) = 0.1616 over 5000 trials; five towers at 0.85 + 4 x 0.30 on n=30 gives 0.0258. (3) Winner's curse: with all towers truly equal at p=0.700 and n=500, E[S_best_single] - 0.700 = +1.12pp at k=2, +2.11pp at k=4, +2.38pp at k=5, +2.90pp at k=8. (4) Chance inflation: 4-choice multiple choice with towers of literally zero skill, S_best_single is about 0.250 while S_oracle = 1 - 0.75^k = 0.438/0.578/0.684/0.763 for k = 2/3/4/5; five zero-skill towers manufacture a 0.513-wide denominator out of independent guessing. (5) Even benign: five independent towers at p=0.70 give S_oracle = 1 - 0.3^5 = 0.99757, so reaching eta = 1 requires the router to identify per item which tower happens to be correct, i.e. to already know the answer.

**One sub-claim refuted, recorded.** The objection that "the stated reading eta = 0 means the router adds nothing is wrong" does not hold. `logos.tex:500` says "At eta = 0 the router adds nothing over picking one tower **and keeping it**", and a fixed-best-tower policy scores exactly 0 by construction. The negative scores found in simulation (mean -0.0876, p5 -0.219, min -0.392 for a uniformly random router over 5 equal towers at p=0.70, n=500) belong to a *random* router, which is not that policy. What survives of that objection is only that eta is unbounded below with no stated scale.

**Fix.** Define eta only when S_oracle - S_best_single > delta for a stated delta, and report "degenerate, one tower dominates" otherwise, which is itself the actionable answer. State that eta is unbounded below and clip or report raw. Estimate the floor on a held-out split from the split used for S_router, or apply a max-of-k debiasing correction, and report the correction size. Chance-correct the ceiling. Then restate F10's criterion numerically instead of "recovering less than most of its pre-swap routing efficiency eta", which as written is unmeasurable when eta can be negative or undefined.

## 7. Findings from the forward-work tracks, at a lower evidentiary tier

Four design tracks were run alongside the referee pass. Their findings did **not** go through the independent refutation step, so they are reported separately and at a lower tier. They are listed because three of them are cheap and consequential.

- **F2 may already be falsified by the paper's own cited source, at zero GPU cost.** `logos.tex:679` states the F2 falsifying observation as "Composed ensemble scoring below a matched jointly post-trained baseline at any scale from 7B upward". `logos.tex:181` reports BAR's own numbers: 49.1 aggregate against retraining baselines of 47.8 without mid-training and 50.5 with. BAR itself performs per-expert mid-training, so the matched joint baseline is the 50.5 arm, and 49.1 < 50.5. `logos.tex:183` even concedes "Branch-Adapt-Route matches a retraining baseline; it does not beat one" without noticing this satisfies F2 as written. F2 is mis-specified as a level test; a level test at one scale cannot constrain a 400x extrapolation. Recommended rewrite: a trend test on the composition gap Delta(N) = score_composed(N) - score_joint(N), falsified by Delta decreasing monotonically in N, with Delta(7e9) = -1.4 recorded as the existing measurement. Cost of a three-point ladder at 1B/7B/24B, both arms: about 4.24e22 FLOPs, about 40,000 H100-hours, $79k to $131k at current rental rates. A 1B pilot rung alone is about 1,240 H100-hours, $2.5k to $4.1k.
- **Extrapolating the joint MoE scaling law suggests D_opt for a 2.8T/50B-active tower is about 3.17e11 tokens, not 5.6e13.** An implementation of Ludziejewski et al. 2025 (arXiv:2502.05172, the paper's own cited law) from its Table 3 coefficients reproduces their published Table 1 exactly at F=1e22 (E=1 gives 18.8B/88.6B; E=32 gives 12.2B/136.9B), then returns 3.167e11 for the K3-like configuration, i.e. 6.33 tokens per active parameter. This is a roughly 20x extrapolation beyond that paper's fitting range and is not independently refuted, so it is reported as a bracket: [3.2e11 compute-optimal, 1.0e12 Chinchilla-on-active, 2.0e13 to 2.4e13 at DeepSeek-V3 and Kimi-K2 over-training practice]. If it holds, F1 resolves on a laptop and resolves against the paper.
- **The unique-data ceiling has a standard citation and it is absent.** Villalobos, Ho, Sevilla, Besiroglu, Heim, Hobbhahn, arXiv:2211.04325v2 (ICML 2024 Position track): effective stock about 300T with 90% CI [100T, 1000T], where "effective" embeds a 5x epoch multiplier; backing that out gives a unique quality-filtered stock of about 60T [20T, 200T]. So `logos.tex:94`'s 200T is not outside "any plausible estimate"; it sits at the 90% upper bound. This is the concrete form of the X-13 and X-04 gap.
- **The F9 harness spec has a gradient-flow defect that would guarantee a false negative before any GPU runs.** `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:193` masks the loss on all observation codes, and in the §5.3 Substrate-A schema the result frame is the last span of the trace (`result.hp_delta` is "PROBE LABELS ONLY" at line 218; `yield` is a curation scalar). Under causal attention an input embedding at position i receives gradient only from loss-bearing positions j>i, and `:262` uses FlexAttention block masks to forbid cross-document attention, so the gradient to every result-frame code is identically zero. The cited support does not transfer: PaliGemma arXiv:2407.07726 §5.2 applies loss "on the suffix (the output) only", where the masked image is the *prefix* and is always followed by loss. Here the masked span is last. Fix: add a mandatory loss-bearing `outcome` span after every result frame and make it a compiler assertion that no trace ends on a loss-masked span.
- **The F9 held-out vocabulary table cannot measure its own primary endpoint.** `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:131` sets the control moves to `tackle` and `scratch`, both Normal-type, and Generation-I Normal-type moves have no super-effective matchup (Bulbapedia Gen-I chart: 1x against everything except 0.5x vs Rock and 0x vs Ghost). The only Fire move, `ember`, is on the held-out side. So the control condition of the "does the model choose super-effective moves above chance versus the text-supported controls" probe can never register the measured quantity, while `:134` states "The control set is not optional. It is what makes a null result interpretable."
- **The F9 throughput figure exceeds the stated card's hardware ceiling.** `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:258` gives 18k-30k tok/s at 350M/ctx2048 on an RTX 3090. At that config the model costs 2.526 GFLOP/token forward+backward, so 30k tok/s requires 75.8 TFLOP/s sustained. The NVIDIA GA102 whitepaper Appendix A Table 9 (p.44, fetched) gives the RTX 3090 FE a peak BF16 tensor throughput with FP32 accumulate of 71 TFLOPS dense (142 only with 2:4 structural sparsity, which does not apply). The upper bound requires above 100% MFU. The same table lists FP16/BF16/TF32/INT8/INT4 rows and no FP8 row, which independently confirms `:254`'s "FP8 does not [work on Ampere]". Derived replacement: 9.1k tok/s at 350M (band 6.6-9.3k for 25-35% MFU), 29k at 125M.
- **F9 as budgeted buys one seed per arm and therefore cannot return the negative it promises.** `GAPS.md:33` budgets "3-4 days training" = 72-96 GPU-h; at the spec's own throughput a 2.0e9-token run is 23.15 GPU-h, so the budget buys 3.1 to 4.2 runs across four arms. With n=1 no variance estimate exists and no test statistic can be computed. PolyPythias (arXiv:2503.09543) reports a 20% per-run outlier rate at 410M ("only two such combinations: 410M seed 3 and 4"), so at n=1 across five arms P(at least one arm corrupted) = 1 - 0.8^5 = 0.672. And `LOGOS_HARNESS.md:307` stakes falsification on "matches", which is an equivalence claim: the TOST margin at 80% power is 2.486 sigma at n=2 and 1.243 sigma at n=8, while the exact Jonckheere-Terpstra permutation floor for a perfectly separated 4-arm ordering is 3.97e-4 at n=2 and 2.71e-6 at n=3. The positive is cheap and the negative is not, which is the exact inverse of the purpose `GAPS.md:111` assigns F9. A powered redesign (n=8 at 125M as the screen, n=3 at 350M as confirmatory, plus a 5-round collapse sub-study and a 20% outlier reserve) is about 718 GPU-h, $145-180 rented or about EUR 76 of electricity on an owned 3090.

- **Substrate B's observation operators exist; its observation data does not.** `.gitignore:56` excludes `validation/**/data/` wholesale and `find validation -type d -name data` returns zero directories; the 7.1 GB Reddit dump five harvesters depend on is not in the tree. Entity scrubbing raises the floor but cannot strip structural recognisability. The block-label shuffle (`neff_collapse_wsb.py:275-285`, 300 permutations preserving users, volumes and graph while destroying block structure) and the matched-calm arm (`roster_wsb.py:53`) generate episodes that never happened and therefore cannot be memorised, at 300 shuffles x 58 episodes.
